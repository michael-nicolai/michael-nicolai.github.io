Research

I’m an evolutionary biologist who never lost his curiosity. I like to look around in the field, but mostly in natural history collections, and wonder “why?”. My research reflects this curiosity and is focused on how and why phenotypes evolve.

I do this using a variety of techniques including phylogenetic comparative analyses, wind tunnel testing, phenotype characterization, and others. My work broadly explores how colour, structure, function, ecology and evolutionary history interact to generate phenotypic diversity.

GENESIS — The GENomics and Evolution of Structural Iridescent Signals

FWO funded

Iridescent colours are responsible for some of the most brilliant and diverse colours in the animal kingdom. Paradoxically, iridescence remains one of the least understood mechanisms responsible for bird colouration.

Unlike pigment-based colouration, where colour is produced by absorption of light, iridescent colouration is created by the scattering of light by photonic structures. Pigment organelles called melanosomes form important building blocks of these structures.

The goal of GENESIS is to link genotype, phenotype and the evolution of iridescent colours. The project is now finishing, so watch out for more shiny publications soon.

Trait-based biogeography

You know those songs that get stuck in your head? Biogeography is one of those for me. Reflecting my early ventures into science, I have a weak spot for biogeography and the models they use.

Colour space? Looks like it has continents and islands to be populated by species. The evolution of traits? Might as well influence dispersal. While this is not my main research field, I am able to continue working on it through great collaborators.

Thinking about the future of colour science

Science is inherently dynamic, building on previous work. Apart from my main research, I also like to conceptualize the future of our research field.

I do this through reviews and perspectives identifying what has been done and, more importantly, what should be done in the future.
